CPM M4 steel

CPM M4 is a powder metallurgy high-speed steel from Crucible. It rates 8/10 for edge retention, 5/10 for toughness and 2/10 for corrosion resistance, and is usually hardened to 62–65 HRC.

What makes CPM M4 behave the way it does

CPM M4 contains 1.42% carbon, a high level that supports high hardness and a large volume of hard carbides; 4% chromium, too little for stainless behaviour.

Its 4% vanadium forms vanadium carbides, among the hardest carbides in knife steels, which drive wear resistance and slow edge loss but also make the steel slower to sharpen; tungsten (5.5%) adds hard carbides and helps the steel keep its hardness; molybdenum (5.25%) improves hardenability.

It is made by powder metallurgy: the alloy is atomised into fine powder and consolidated, which keeps the carbides small and evenly spread. That is what lets a highly alloyed steel stay tough enough for a knife blade.

Popular competition-cutting steel; excellent toughness for its wear resistance.

How to sharpen CPM M4

CPM M4 is slow to sharpen (3/10). Its vanadium and tungsten carbides are harder than the aluminium oxide in conventional stones, so diamond or CBN plates cut it far more efficiently. Once sharp, it needs touch-ups less often.

FAQ

Is CPM M4 a good knife steel?

CPM M4 is strongest in edge retention (8/10) and weakest in corrosion resistance (2/10). Its average across the four ratings is 4.5/10, and it sits in the premium price tier.

Does CPM M4 rust?

Yes. CPM M4 rates 2/10 and will rust or patina without care; dry it after use and keep a thin coat of oil on it.

Is CPM M4 hard to sharpen?

CPM M4 is slow to sharpen (3/10). Its vanadium and tungsten carbides are harder than the aluminium oxide in conventional stones, so diamond or CBN plates cut it far more efficiently. Once sharp, it needs touch-ups less often.

What hardness is CPM M4?

Knives in CPM M4 are usually hardened to 62–65 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
