VG-10 steel

VG-10 is a conventional stainless steel from Takefu Special Steel. It rates 5/10 for edge retention, 4/10 for toughness and 7/10 for corrosion resistance, and is usually hardened to 59–61 HRC.

What makes VG-10 behave the way it does

VG-10 contains 1% carbon, enough to reach typical knife hardness; 15% chromium for corrosion resistance.

A small vanadium addition (0.2%) mainly refines grain size; molybdenum (1%) improves hardenability and pitting resistance; cobalt (1.5%) strengthens the matrix.

Cobalt improves hardenability; prone to micro-chipping if ground too thin.

How to sharpen VG-10

VG-10 is moderately easy to sharpen (6/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

FAQ

Is VG-10 a good knife steel?

VG-10 is strongest in corrosion resistance (7/10) and weakest in toughness (4/10). Its average across the four ratings is 5.5/10, and it sits in the mid-range price tier.

Does VG-10 rust?

Not in normal use. VG-10 rates 7/10 for corrosion resistance; wipe it dry after contact with salt or acidic food.

Is VG-10 hard to sharpen?

VG-10 is moderately easy to sharpen (6/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

What hardness is VG-10?

Knives in VG-10 are usually hardened to 59–61 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
