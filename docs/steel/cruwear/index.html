What makes CruWear behave the way it does

CPM CruWear contains 1.1% carbon, enough to reach typical knife hardness; 7.5% chromium, too little for stainless behaviour.

Its 2.4% vanadium forms vanadium carbides, among the hardest carbides in knife steels, which drive wear resistance and slow edge loss but also make the steel slower to sharpen; tungsten (1.15%) adds hard carbides and helps the steel keep its hardness; molybdenum (1.6%) improves hardenability.

It is made by powder metallurgy: the alloy is atomised into fine powder and consolidated, which keeps the carbides small and evenly spread. That is what lets a highly alloyed steel stay tough enough for a knife blade.

Descendant of Vasco Wear; Z-Wear (Zapp) is the same composition.

How to sharpen CruWear

CruWear is moderately easy to sharpen (5/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

FAQ

Is CruWear a good knife steel?

CruWear is strongest in edge retention (7/10) and weakest in corrosion resistance (3/10). Its average across the four ratings is 5.5/10, and it sits in the premium price tier.

Does CruWear rust?

Yes, if neglected. CruWear rates only 3/10; keep it dry and lightly oiled, especially in humid weather.

Is CruWear hard to sharpen?

CruWear is moderately easy to sharpen (5/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

What hardness is CruWear?

Knives in CruWear are usually hardened to 60–64 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
