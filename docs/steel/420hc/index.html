420HC steel

420HC is a conventional stainless steel. It rates 2/10 for edge retention, 7/10 for toughness and 7/10 for corrosion resistance, and is usually hardened to 56–58 HRC.

What makes 420HC behave the way it does

420HC contains 0.46% carbon, a low level that favours toughness and corrosion resistance; 13% chromium for corrosion resistance.

A small vanadium addition (0.3%) mainly refines grain size.

Buck's Paul Bos heat treat gets the most out of it. Fine-grained, very easy to sharpen.

How to sharpen 420HC

420HC is easy to sharpen (9/10). Aluminium-oxide and water stones work well, and it takes a fine edge quickly; a ceramic rod or strop keeps it going between sharpenings.

FAQ

Is 420HC a good knife steel?

420HC is strongest in ease of sharpening (9/10) and weakest in edge retention (2/10). Its average across the four ratings is 6.2/10, and it sits in the budget price tier.

Does 420HC rust?

Not in normal use. 420HC rates 7/10 for corrosion resistance; wipe it dry after contact with salt or acidic food.

Is 420HC hard to sharpen?

420HC is easy to sharpen (9/10). Aluminium-oxide and water stones work well, and it takes a fine edge quickly; a ceramic rod or strop keeps it going between sharpenings.

What hardness is 420HC?

Knives in 420HC are usually hardened to 56–58 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
