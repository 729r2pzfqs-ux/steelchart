N690 steel

N690 is a conventional stainless steel from voestalpine Bohler Edelstahl. It rates 5/10 for edge retention, 4/10 for toughness and 8/10 for corrosion resistance, and is usually hardened to 58–60 HRC.

What makes N690 behave the way it does

N690 contains 1.07% carbon, enough to reach typical knife hardness; 17% chromium for corrosion resistance.

A small vanadium addition (0.1%) mainly refines grain size; molybdenum (1.1%) improves hardenability and pitting resistance; cobalt (1.5%) strengthens the matrix.

European VG-10/440C class steel; staple of Italian knives.

How to sharpen N690

N690 is moderately easy to sharpen (6/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

FAQ

Is N690 a good knife steel?

N690 is strongest in corrosion resistance (8/10) and weakest in toughness (4/10). Its average across the four ratings is 5.8/10, and it sits in the mid-range price tier.

Does N690 rust?

Not in normal use. N690 rates 8/10 for corrosion resistance; wipe it dry after contact with salt or acidic food.

Is N690 hard to sharpen?

N690 is moderately easy to sharpen (6/10). Water stones and ceramics work; diamond plates speed up reprofiling or repairing a damaged edge.

What hardness is N690?

Knives in N690 are usually hardened to 58–60 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
