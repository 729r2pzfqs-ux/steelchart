ZDP-189 steel

ZDP-189 is a powder metallurgy stainless steel from Hitachi Metals. It rates 9/10 for edge retention, 2/10 for toughness and 5/10 for corrosion resistance, and is usually hardened to 64–67 HRC.

What makes ZDP-189 behave the way it does

ZDP-189 contains 3% carbon, a high level that supports high hardness and a large volume of hard carbides; 20% chromium for corrosion resistance.

A small vanadium addition (0.1%) mainly refines grain size; molybdenum (1.4%) improves hardenability and pitting resistance.

It is made by powder metallurgy: the alloy is atomised into fine powder and consolidated, which keeps the carbides small and evenly spread. That is what lets a highly alloyed steel stay tough enough for a knife blade.

Most Cr tied up in carbides, so only moderately stainless despite 20% Cr. Usually laminated.

How to sharpen ZDP-189

ZDP-189 is slow to sharpen (2/10). Its large volume of hard carbides makes conventional stones slow going; diamond plates cut it much faster. Once sharp, it needs touch-ups less often.

FAQ

Is ZDP-189 a good knife steel?

ZDP-189 is strongest in edge retention (9/10) and weakest in ease of sharpening (2/10). Its average across the four ratings is 4.5/10, and it sits in the ultra-premium price tier.

Does ZDP-189 rust?

It can. ZDP-189 rates 5/10: stain-resistant rather than rust-proof, so it can spot if left wet or salty.

Is ZDP-189 hard to sharpen?

ZDP-189 is slow to sharpen (2/10). Its large volume of hard carbides makes conventional stones slow going; diamond plates cut it much faster. Once sharp, it needs touch-ups less often.

What hardness is ZDP-189?

Knives in ZDP-189 are usually hardened to 64–67 HRC. Makers can run it outside that range; harder means better edge retention but lower toughness.
