Steel types

Stainless steels11 steelsConventional (ingot) stainless steels with enough free chromium, roughly 12% or more, to resist rust in everyday use.Powder metallurgy stainless steels11 steelsStainless steels made by powder metallurgy, which keeps carbides small and evenly spread so high-alloy steels stay tough enough for knives.Tool steels1 steelsIngot tool steels designed for dies and cutting tools; high wear resistance, limited corrosion resistance.Powder metallurgy tool steels2 steelsPowder metallurgy tool steels that combine high toughness with wear resistance, with little corrosion resistance.High-speed powder steels2 steelsPowder metallurgy high-speed steels with tungsten, molybdenum and vanadium; very high hardness and edge retention, low corrosion resistance.Carbon steels3 steelsSimple carbon and low-alloy steels: easy to sharpen and often tough, but they rust and patina without care.
