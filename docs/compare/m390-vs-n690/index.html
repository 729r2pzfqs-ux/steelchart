Property by property

Edge retention

M390 holds an edge longer: 8/10 against 5/10 for N690, a 3-point gap. Vanadium: 4% vs 0.1%; more vanadium carbide generally means more wear resistance.

Toughness

Both rate 4/10 for toughness. Typical hardness: 60–62 vs 58–60 HRC; harder blades trade toughness for edge holding.

Corrosion resistance

M390 resists corrosion better: 9/10 against 8/10 for N690, a 1-point gap. Chromium: 20% vs 17%, though what matters is the chromium left in solution, not the total.

Ease of sharpening

N690 is easier to sharpen: 6/10 against 3/10 for M390, a 3-point gap.

FAQ

Which is better, M390 or N690?

M390 holds an edge longer and resists corrosion better; N690 is easier to sharpen. Choose M390 for edge retention, N690 for easy sharpening.

Which is harder, M390 or N690?

M390 is typically run at 60–62 HRC and N690 at 58–60 HRC.

Is M390 or N690 more rust resistant?

M390 (9/10). M390 is a powder metallurgy stainless steel; N690 is a conventional stainless steel.
