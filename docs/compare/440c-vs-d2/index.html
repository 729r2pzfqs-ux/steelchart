Edge retention

D2 holds an edge longer: 6/10 against 4/10 for 440C, a 2-point gap. Vanadium: 0% vs 0.9%; more vanadium carbide generally means more wear resistance.

Toughness

Both rate 3/10 for toughness. Typical hardness: 57–59 vs 58–61 HRC; harder blades trade toughness for edge holding.

Corrosion resistance

440C resists corrosion better: 7/10 against 4/10 for D2, a 3-point gap. Chromium: 17% vs 12%, though what matters is the chromium left in solution, not the total.

FAQ

Which is better, 440C or D2?

440C resists corrosion better and is easier to sharpen; D2 holds an edge longer. Choose 440C for corrosion resistance, D2 for edge retention.

Which is harder, 440C or D2?

440C is typically run at 57–59 HRC and D2 at 58–61 HRC.

Is 440C or D2 more rust resistant?

440C (7/10). 440C is a conventional stainless steel; D2 is a tool steel.
