Property by property

Edge retention

D2 holds an edge longer: 6/10 against 3/10 for Nitro-V, a 3-point gap. Vanadium: 0.9% vs 0.08%; more vanadium carbide generally means more wear resistance.

Toughness

Nitro-V is tougher: 8/10 against 3/10 for D2, a 5-point gap. Typical hardness: 58–61 vs 59–62 HRC; harder blades trade toughness for edge holding.

Corrosion resistance

Nitro-V resists corrosion better: 7/10 against 4/10 for D2, a 3-point gap. Chromium: 12% vs 13%, though what matters is the chromium left in solution, not the total.

Ease of sharpening

Nitro-V is easier to sharpen: 9/10 against 4/10 for D2, a 5-point gap.

FAQ

Which is better, D2 or Nitro-V?

D2 holds an edge longer; Nitro-V is tougher, resists corrosion better and is easier to sharpen. Choose D2 for edge retention, Nitro-V for toughness.

Which is harder, D2 or Nitro-V?

D2 is typically run at 58–61 HRC and Nitro-V at 59–62 HRC.

Is D2 or Nitro-V more rust resistant?

Nitro-V (7/10). D2 is a tool steel; Nitro-V is a conventional stainless steel.
