Property by property

Edge retention

S35VN holds an edge longer: 6/10 against 3/10 for Nitro-V, a 3-point gap. Vanadium: 0.08% vs 3%; more vanadium carbide generally means more wear resistance.

Toughness

Nitro-V is tougher: 8/10 against 5/10 for S35VN, a 3-point gap. Typical hardness: 59–62 vs 58–61 HRC; harder blades trade toughness for edge holding.

Corrosion resistance

Both rate 7/10 for corrosion resistance. Chromium: 13% vs 14%, though what matters is the chromium left in solution, not the total.

Ease of sharpening

Nitro-V is easier to sharpen: 9/10 against 5/10 for S35VN, a 4-point gap.

FAQ

Which is better, Nitro-V or S35VN?

Nitro-V is tougher and is easier to sharpen; S35VN holds an edge longer. Choose Nitro-V for easy sharpening, S35VN for edge retention.

Which is harder, Nitro-V or S35VN?

Nitro-V is typically run at 59–62 HRC and S35VN at 58–61 HRC.

Is Nitro-V or S35VN more rust resistant?

Neither: both rate 7/10. Nitro-V is a conventional stainless steel; S35VN is a powder metallurgy stainless steel.
