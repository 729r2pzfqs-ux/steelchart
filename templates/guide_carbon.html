What makes a steel stainless

Steel becomes stainless when there is enough chromium dissolved in it, roughly 12% or more, to form a thin, self-repairing chromium oxide film on the surface. Chromium that ends up locked in carbides does not help, which is why D2 with about 12% chromium is only semi-stainless. Carbon steels have little or no chromium, so iron oxide (rust) forms whenever the blade stays wet.

Why choose carbon steel

  • Sharpening: simple carbon steels take a very keen edge quickly on any stone.
  • Toughness for the price: spring and tool grades such as 80CrV2 and 1095 handle chopping well.
  • Patina: a grey-blue oxide layer forms with use and slows further rust. Many kitchen-knife and bushcraft users like it.

Why choose stainless

  • Low maintenance: it tolerates sweat, humidity, food acids and dishwater far better.
  • Edge retention: powder stainless steels such as M390 and S90V hold an edge far longer than simple carbon steels.
  • Modern balance: steels such as MagnaCut, AEB-L and 14C28N now match many carbon steels for toughness while staying rust-resistant.
